# Facilitator Guide: Washback Check

## Learning objectives

By the end of the activity, learners should be able to:

1. distinguish positive from negative washback in language assessment contexts;
2. justify a judgment by connecting assessment features to teaching and learning behavior; and
3. identify an assessment redesign that can promote more beneficial washback.

## Suggested lesson flow (25–35 minutes)

1. **Activate prior knowledge (3 minutes).** Ask: “What changed in the way you studied because of an important test?” Collect one helpful and one harmful effect.
2. **Clarify the concept (4 minutes).** Use the opening screen: washback concerns the influence a test has on teaching and learning. Stress that a format is not automatically positive or negative; consequences depend on alignment, use, stakes, scoring, preparation, and context.
3. **Individual or paired challenge (12–18 minutes).** Learners complete seven classification items and two application challenges. For each negative classification situation, they must also identify the best repair. Learners may revisit any item using the numbered navigator.
4. **Debrief (6–10 minutes).** Discuss two difficult cases. Ask learners to identify (a) the intended construct, (b) the behavior the assessment encouraged, and (c) the alignment or mismatch between them.

## Scoring

- 7 points: correct positive/negative classifications
- 3 points: correct redesigns for negative-washback situations
- 2 points: correct application decisions
- 12 points total

Suggested interpretation for a formative knowledge check:

- 10–12: Strong conceptual understanding
- 7–9: Developing understanding; discuss missed rationales
- 0–6: Revisit the decision rule and work through examples together

These ranges are instructional, not validated cut scores. Avoid using them for a high-stakes grade without local review and evidence.

## Answer guide

| Item | Classification | Main reason / preferred repair |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Positive | Authentic interaction and scoring criteria align with speaking goals. |
| 2 | Negative | Choose the second option: add planning, drafting, revising, and source-based writing aligned with academic-writing outcomes. |
| 3 | Positive | Learners practice transferable interaction strategies rather than one script. |
| 4 | Negative | Choose the third option: sample main ideas, inference, evaluation, and authentic reading responses—not only details. |
| 5 | Positive | Low-stakes evidence informs targeted reteaching and learner monitoring. |
| 6 | Negative | Choose the first option: weight oral delivery, meaning, interaction, and audience awareness appropriately. |
| 7 | Positive | Vocabulary learning transfers to a meaningful community task. |
| 8 | Application | Choose the third option: a paired problem-solving task scored for meaning, interaction, intelligibility, and strategy use. |
| 9 | Application | Choose the second option: audit the construct, broaden specifications and sampling, align scoring, and monitor classroom effects. |

## Debrief prompts

- What exactly is the assessment intended to measure?
- What teaching or study behavior does it encourage?
- Is that behavior likely to improve the intended language ability?
- Who benefits or is disadvantaged by the assessment practice?
- Which change would improve alignment without making scoring impractical?

## Technical notes

The activity runs entirely in the browser. Answers are not sent to a teacher or server. If evidence of completion is needed, ask learners to print/save the results page as a PDF or show the completed screen.
